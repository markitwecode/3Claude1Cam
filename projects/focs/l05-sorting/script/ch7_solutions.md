[c7.bubcost.2] {pass} Call one sweep from left to right a pass. [c7.bubcode.2] {two} One pass returns two things, the new list, and whether it swapped anything. {code} We'll call it bee-pass. {pairs} A list with at least two items starts with ex and why. {inorder} If ex is at most why, ex stays in front, the pass carries on from why, and we keep its answer about swaps. {swap} Otherwise, they swap. Why goes in front, ex is carried on, and the answer is true. {short} A shorter list is already in order, and nothing swaps.

[c7.bubcode.3] {carried} Carrying ex forward is how the largest item rides to the end. {ptype} Bee-pass takes an alpha list, and returns an alpha list paired with a bool. {bubble} Bubble sort runs one pass. {again} If it swapped, it goes again, on the new list. {stop2} If not, the list is sorted. {btype} It's alpha list to alpha list. {tail} The repeat is the last thing bubble does, so it's iterative.

[c7.bubcode.4] {trace} On our list, the first pass gives three, five, four, seven, nine, and reports a swap. {p2} The second pass gives the sorted list, and reports a swap again. {p3} The third changes nothing and reports false, so bubble returns the sorted list. {dups} With repeats, three, five, three, one, five becomes one, three, three, five, five, in four passes. {pause}

[c7.bubcode.5] {slip} Now the tempting slip. In the test, write less than, instead of less than or equal. {chainok} On our list, with no repeats, it still works. {equal} But now two equal neighbours count as out of order, so they swap, and the pass reports a swap. {forever} On three, five, three, one, five, three passes reach the sorted list. {never} After that, every pass swaps the two threes and the two fives, reports true, and goes round again, forever. {pause}

[c7.bubcode.6] {hang} The toplevel never replies. {interrupt} Interrupt it, and all it says is, interrupted. {rule} So swap only when ex is strictly greater than why. {test} And, as with selection sort, always test with repeats.
